    1.

    Show that the following limit does not exist
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{3xy}{2x^2+5y^2}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let
    \begin{equation*} f(x,y)=\frac{3xy}{2x^2+5y^2}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Method 1 (the limit along two paths).
    Along \(y=0\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{x\to 0} f(x,0)=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{0}{2x^2}=0\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Along \(y=x\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \lim_{x\to 0} f(x,x) \amp = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{3x^2}{2x^2+5x^2}\\ \amp = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{3x^2}{7x^2}\\ \amp = \frac{3}{7}\text{.} \end{align*}
    Since \(0\neq\frac{3}{7}\text{,}\) the limit does not exist.
    🔗
    Method 2 (the limit along all lines \(y=mx\)).
    \begin{align*} \lim_{x\to 0} f(x,mx) \amp = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{3mx^2}{2x^2+5m^2x^2}\\ \amp = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{3m}{2+5m^2}\\ \amp = \frac{3m}{2+5m^2}\text{.} \end{align*}
    This value depends on \(m\text{,}\) so the limit does not exist.
    🔗

    2.

    Find the locations of any local maxima, local minima, and saddle points of \(f(x,y)=x^3+y^3-3xy\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The first partial derivatives are
    \begin{align*} f_x(x,y) \amp = 3x^2-3y\\ f_y(x,y) \amp = 3y^2-3x\text{.} \end{align*}
    Setting \(f_x(x,y)=0\) gives \(y=x^2\text{.}\) Substituting into \(f_y(x,y)=0\) gives
    \begin{equation*} x^4-x = x(x^3-1) = 0\text{,} \end{equation*}
    so \(x=0\) or \(x=1\text{.}\) The critical points are \((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The second partial derivatives are
    \begin{align*} f_{xx}(x,y) \amp = 6x\\ f_{xy}(x,y) \amp = -3\\ f_{yy}(x,y) \amp = 6y\text{,} \end{align*}
    so
    \begin{align*} D(x,y) \amp = (6x)(6y)-(-3)^2\\ \amp = 36xy-9\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    • \(D(0,0)=36(0)(0)-9=-9\lt 0\text{,}\) so \((0,0)\) is a saddle point.
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(D(1,1)=36(1)(1)-9=27\gt 0\) and \(f_{xx}(1,1)=6\gt 0\text{,}\) so \((1,1)\) is a local minimum.
      🔗
      🔗
    • There are no local maxima.
      🔗
      🔗

    3.

    Let
    \begin{equation*} f(x,y)=x\,e^{xy}+\frac{x}{1+y^2}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Compute the mixed partial derivatives \(f_{xy}\) and \(f_{yx}\text{,}\) and verify that Clairaut’s theorem holds.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the product rule and chain rule on the first term and the quotient rule on the second term:
    \begin{align*} f_x(x,y) \amp = (1)\,e^{xy}+x\,(y\,e^{xy})+\frac{(1)(1+y^2)-x(0)}{(1+y^2)^2}\\ \amp = (1+xy)\,e^{xy}+\frac{1}{1+y^2} \end{align*}
    \begin{align*} f_y(x,y) \amp = x\,(x\,e^{xy})+\frac{(0)(1+y^2)-x(2y)}{(1+y^2)^2}\\ \amp = x^2e^{xy}-\frac{2xy}{(1+y^2)^2}\text{.} \end{align*}
    Differentiating \(f_x(x,y)\) with respect to \(y\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} f_{xy}(x,y) \amp = (x)\,e^{xy}+(1+xy)(x\,e^{xy})+\frac{(0)(1+y^2)-(1)(2y)}{(1+y^2)^2}\\ \amp = (2x+x^2y)\,e^{xy}-\frac{2y}{(1+y^2)^2}\text{.} \end{align*}
    Differentiating \(f_y(x,y)\) with respect to \(x\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} f_{yx}(x,y) \amp = 2x\,e^{xy}+x^2(y\,e^{xy})-\frac{2y}{(1+y^2)^2}\\ \amp = (2x+x^2y)\,e^{xy}-\frac{2y}{(1+y^2)^2}\text{.} \end{align*}
    Therefore \(f_{xy}(x,y)=f_{yx}(x,y)\text{.}\)

    5.

    Let \(f(x,y)=\sqrt{x^2+3y}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the equation of the tangent plane to the graph \(z=f(x,y)\) at the point \((1,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We have \(f(1,1)=\sqrt{1+3}=2\text{.}\) So, \((1,1,2)\) is a point on the plane. The partial derivatives are
    \begin{align*} f_x(x,y) \amp = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+3y}}\\ f_y(x,y) \amp = \frac{3}{2\sqrt{x^2+3y}}\text{.} \end{align*}
    So, \(f_x(1,1)=\frac{1}{2}\) and \(f_y(1,1)=\frac{3}{4}\text{,}\) and hence
    \begin{equation*} \bvec{n}=\langle f_x(1,1),\,f_y(1,1),\,-1\rangle=\left\langle \tfrac{1}{2},\,\tfrac{3}{4},\,-1\right\rangle \end{equation*}
    is a normal vector to the tangent plane. So, the equation of the tangent plane is
    \begin{equation*} \tfrac{1}{2}(x-1)+\tfrac{3}{4}(y-1)-(z-2)=0. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the linearization \(L(x,y)\) of \(f\) at the point \((1,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The linearization is the function whose graph is the tangent plane:
    \begin{equation*} L(x,y)=2+\tfrac{1}{2}(x-1)+\tfrac{3}{4}(y-1)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Use your answer to part (b) to approximate \(f(1.04,\,0.96)\text{.}\) Then use a calculator to find the actual value, and compare.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} f(1.04,\,0.96) \approx L(1.04,\,0.96) \amp = 2+\tfrac{1}{2}(0.04)+\tfrac{3}{4}(-0.04)\\ \amp = 2+0.02-0.03\\ \amp = 1.99\text{.} \end{align*}
    The actual value is \(f(1.04,\,0.96)=\sqrt{3.9616}\approx 1.9904\text{,}\) so the approximation is off by only about \(0.0004\text{.}\)

    6.

    You are hiking on a hill whose elevation (in meters) is
    \begin{equation*} h(x,y)=1200-0.005x^2-0.01y^2 \end{equation*}
    where \(x\) and \(y\) are measured in meters, the positive \(x\)-axis points east, and the positive \(y\)-axis points north. You are standing at the point \((60,20)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    If you walk northeast, are you ascending or descending? At what rate?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The gradient is
    \begin{equation*} \grad h(x,y)=\langle -0.01x,\,-0.02y\rangle\text{.} \end{equation*}
    So,
    \begin{equation*} \grad h(60,20)=\langle -0.6,\,-0.4\rangle\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Northeast is the direction of the unit vector \(\bvec{u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\langle 1,1\rangle\text{,}\) so
    \begin{align*} D_{\bvec{u}}h(60,20) \amp = \langle -0.6,\,-0.4\rangle\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\langle 1,1\rangle\\ \amp = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = -\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\text{.} \end{align*}
    Since the directional derivative is negative, you are descending, at a rate of \(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\approx 0.71\) meters of elevation per meter walked.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    In what direction should you walk to climb most steeply? (You do not have to give compass directions; a vector is sufficient.) What is the slope in that direction?
    🔗
    Solution.
    You climb most steeply in the direction of \(\grad h(60,20)=\langle -0.6,\,-0.4\rangle\) (roughly southwest, but more west than south). The slope in this direction is
    \begin{equation*} |\grad h(60,20)|=\sqrt{(-0.6)^2+(-0.4)^2}=\sqrt{0.52}\approx 0.72\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    In what direction(s) should you walk to stay at the same elevation?
    🔗
    Solution.
    To stay at the same elevation, walk in a direction \(\bvec{u}\) with \(D_{\bvec{u}}h(60,20)=\grad h(60,20)\cdot\bvec{u}=0\text{,}\) that is, perpendicular to \(\langle -0.6,-0.4\rangle\text{.}\) Two such vectors are
    \begin{equation*} \bvec{v}=\langle 0.4,-0.6\rangle \quad\text{and}\quad \bvec{w}=\langle -0.4,0.6\rangle\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗

    7.

    A rectangular box without a lid is to be made from \(12\text{ m}^2\) of cardboard. Find the maximum volume of such a box.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let the base be \(x\) by \(y\) and the height be \(z\) (all in meters). With no lid, the amount of cardboard is \(xy+2xz+2yz=12\text{,}\) so \(z=\frac{12-xy}{2(x+y)}\text{.}\) The volume is
    \begin{equation*} V(x,y)=xyz=\frac{12xy-x^2y^2}{2(x+y)}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Using the quotient rule and simplifying,
    \begin{align*} V_x(x,y) \amp = \frac{(12y-2xy^2)\cdot 2(x+y)-(12xy-x^2y^2)\cdot 2}{4(x+y)^2}\\ \amp = \frac{y^2(12-2xy-x^2)}{2(x+y)^2}\\ V_y(x,y) \amp = \frac{(12x-2x^2y)\cdot 2(x+y)-(12xy-x^2y^2)\cdot 2}{4(x+y)^2}\\ \amp = \frac{x^2(12-2xy-y^2)}{2(x+y)^2}\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Setting both equal to zero gives
    \begin{align*} y^2(12-2xy-x^2) \amp = 0\\ x^2(12-2xy-y^2) \amp = 0\text{.} \end{align*}
    Because \(x,y\gt 0\text{,}\) the solutions \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) are not in the domain, so
    \begin{align*} 12-2xy-x^2 \amp = 0\\ 12-2xy-y^2 \amp = 0\text{.} \end{align*}
    Subtracting the equations, we obtain \(x^2=y^2\text{,}\) and because \(x,y\gt 0\text{,}\) \(x=y\text{.}\) Then \(3x^2=12\text{,}\) so \(x=y=2\) and \(z=\frac{12-4}{8}=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The volume tends to \(0\) at the edges of the allowed region (as \(x\) or \(y\) approaches \(0\text{,}\) or \(xy\) approaches \(12\)), so this single critical point gives the maximum. The maximum volume is \(V=2\cdot 2\cdot 1=4\text{ m}^3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    8.

    Find the absolute maximum and absolute minimum values of
    \begin{align*} f(x,y) \amp = x^2+y^2-6x-2y \end{align*}
    on the closed triangular region with vertices \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((4,0)\text{,}\) and \((4,8)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Since \(f\) is continuous and the region is closed and bounded, the Extreme Value Theorem guarantees that \(f\) has an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum on the region. We find all candidate points.
    🔗
    Interior. The first partial derivatives are
    \begin{align*} f_x(x,y) \amp = 2x-6\\ f_y(x,y) \amp = 2y-2\text{.} \end{align*}
    Setting both equal to zero gives \(x=3\) and \(y=1\text{.}\) Since \(0\lt 3\lt 4\) and \(0\lt 1\lt 2(3)\text{,}\) the critical point \((3,1)\) is inside the triangle.
    🔗
    Bottom side (along \(y=0\)). Here \(f(x,0)=x^2-6x\text{,}\) whose derivative \(2x-6\) is zero at \(x=3\text{.}\) This gives the candidate point \((3,0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Right side (along \(x=4\)). Here \(f(4,y)=y^2-2y-8\text{,}\) whose derivative \(2y-2\) is zero at \(y=1\text{.}\) This gives the candidate point \((4,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hypotenuse (along \(y=2x\)). Here
    \begin{align*} f(x,2x) \amp = x^2+(2x)^2-6x-2(2x)\\ \amp = 5x^2-10x\text{,} \end{align*}
    whose derivative \(10x-10\) is zero at \(x=1\text{.}\) This gives the candidate point \((1,2)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Corner points. The corners \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((4,0)\text{,}\) and \((4,8)\) are also candidates.
    🔗
    
    The shaded triangle with vertices \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((4,0)\text{,}\) and \((4,8)\text{,}\) with the hypotenuse labeled \(y=2x\text{.}\) Seven candidate points are marked: the interior critical point \((3,1)\text{;}\) the side points \((3,0)\) on the bottom, \((4,1)\) on the right side, and \((1,2)\) on the hypotenuse; and the three corners.
    🔗
    Evaluating \(f\) at every candidate:
    🔗
    \((x,y)\) \((3,1)\) \((3,0)\) \((4,1)\) \((1,2)\) \((0,0)\) \((4,0)\) \((4,8)\)
    \(f(x,y)\) \(-10\) \(-9\) \(-9\) \(-5\) \(0\) \(-8\) \(40\)
    The absolute maximum value is \(f(4,8)=40\text{,}\) and the absolute minimum value is \(f(3,1)=-10\text{.}\)
